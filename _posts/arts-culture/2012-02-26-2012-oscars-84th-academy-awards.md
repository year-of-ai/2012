## Summary

The 84th Academy Awards ceremony took place on February 26, 2012, at the Kodak Theatre (renamed Dolby Theatre later that year) in Hollywood, California, honoring cinematic achievements in films released during 2011. The evening's defining narrative was *The Artist*, a French-produced black-and-white silent film directed by Michel Hazanavicius, which became only the second silent film to win Best Picture in Oscar history — the first being *Wings* in 1929. The film swept five awards, with star Jean Dujardin becoming the first French actor to win Best Actor. Meryl Streep won Best Actress for *The Iron Lady*, her 17th nomination and third win. Billy Crystal returned as host for the ninth time, earning praise for an opening montage that inserted him digitally into 2011's nominated films.

## Complete Winners and Notable Nominations

### Picture and Direction

*The Artist* dominated the evening's most prestigious categories:

- **Best Picture**: *The Artist* (Thomas Langmann, producer)
- **Best Director**: Michel Hazanavicius (*The Artist*)
- **Best Actor**: Jean Dujardin (*The Artist*) — first French actor to win the award
- **Best Original Score**: Ludovic Bource (*The Artist*)
- **Best Costume Design**: Mark Bridges (*The Artist*)

Martin Scorsese's *Hugo* led all films with eleven nominations and won five technical awards: Best Cinematography (Robert Richardson), Best Art Direction, Best Film Editing, Best Sound Editing, and Best Sound Mixing. The two films together accounted for ten of the evening's twenty-four awards.

### Acting Awards

- **Best Actress**: Meryl Streep (*The Iron Lady*) — her third competitive Oscar, coming 29 years after her second win for *Sophie's Choice* (at the April 1983 ceremony) and 32 years after her first for *Kramer vs. Kramer* (April 1980)
- **Best Supporting Actor**: Christopher Plummer (*Beginners*), age 82 — became the oldest competitive Academy Award winner in history
- **Best Supporting Actress**: Octavia Spencer (*The Help*)

Notable acting nominees who did not win included George Clooney (*The Descendants*), Brad Pitt (*Moneyball*), and Viola Davis (*The Help*).

### Screenplay and International Film

- **Best Original Screenplay**: Woody Allen (*Midnight in Paris*)
- **Best Adapted Screenplay**: Alexander Payne, Nat Faxon, Jim Rash (*The Descendants*)
- **Best Animated Feature**: *Rango* (Gore Verbinski)
- **Best Foreign Language Film**: *A Separation* (Iran, directed by Asghar Farhadi) — first Iranian film to win in this category

## *The Artist*: Production Context and Historic Achievement

*The Artist* was produced for approximately €15 million (roughly $19 million USD) — a modest budget by Hollywood standards — and shot in Los Angeles over 35 days. Hazanavicius deliberately recreated the visual grammar of 1920s silent cinema, using square-format 1.33:1 aspect ratio, intertitle cards, and a score performed by a live orchestra. The film starred Jean Dujardin as a fictional silent film star and Bérénice Bejo (Hazanavicius's real-life partner) as an actress on the rise in the sound era.

The film premiered at the Cannes Film Festival in May 2011, where Dujardin won Best Actor. Its North American distributor, The Weinstein Company, acquired it for $4.2 million and ran an awards campaign that positioned it as both a tribute to Hollywood's past and a technically demanding feat. *The Artist* earned approximately $133.4 million worldwide against its modest budget — an extraordinary return for a foreign-language, black-and-white silent film.

## Ceremony Details: Viewership and Setting

The ceremony drew approximately 39.3 million viewers in the United States on ABC, up from 37.6 million in 2011 — a three-year high. The red carpet broadcast ran for over two hours before the main ceremony, which lasted approximately 3 hours and 20 minutes. The Kodak Theatre seats 3,400 and was decorated with a black-and-white art deco aesthetic echoing *The Artist*'s visual style.

Billy Crystal's opening involved a pre-filmed sequence in which he appeared alongside actors in scenes from *The Descendants*, *Moneyball*, *Midnight in Paris*, and *Hugo*. He also paid tribute to recently deceased film figures including Elizabeth Taylor, who had died in March 2011.

## Significance

The 2012 Oscars marked an unusual moment in which the Academy's most prestigious honor went to a film that deliberately rejected the dominant technological and commercial conventions of its era. In 2011, major studios were deep into 3D production following *Avatar*'s (2009) record-breaking success; *The Artist*'s win signaled that the Academy's membership — which then numbered approximately 5,765 voting members — valued historical literacy and technical artistry regardless of commercial format.

Christopher Plummer's win at 82 broke the record held by Jessica Tandy, who won Best Actress at age 80 in 1989. Plummer's role in *Beginners* as a man who comes out as gay in his 70s drew considerable attention to the film and its thematic reach. *A Separation*'s win in the Foreign Language Film category — the first for Iran in that category — occurred against a backdrop of heightened US-Iran diplomatic tensions, with the film's director, Asghar Farhadi, using his acceptance speech to address the dignity of Iranian people.
