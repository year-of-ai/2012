## Summary

On 22 May 2012 at 07:44 UTC, SpaceX launched an uncrewed Dragon capsule atop a Falcon 9 v1.0 rocket from Space Launch Complex 40 at Cape Canaveral, Florida. The flight — officially COTS Demo Flight 2, and designated Dragon C2+ because it combined the objectives of two originally separate demonstration missions — was conducted under NASA's Commercial Orbital Transportation Services program, a public-private partnership begun in 2006 to develop privately operated cargo delivery to the International Space Station (ISS).

After a series of orbital maneuvers and rendezvous demonstrations to prove Dragon could operate safely in the station's vicinity, the capsule made its approach on 25 May 2012. Expedition 31 flight engineers Don Pettit and André Kuipers captured Dragon with the station's Canadarm2 robotic arm at 13:56 UTC (9:56 a.m. EDT) and berthed it to the Earth-facing port of the Harmony module about two hours later. Dragon delivered roughly 525 kg (about 1,150 lb) of pressurized cargo — food, water, clothing, and supplies — and, after roughly six days attached to the station, was reloaded with about 665 kg of cargo, including experiment samples and hardware, for return to Earth. The capsule was unberthed and splashed down in the Pacific Ocean off the coast of Baja California on 31 May 2012, where it was recovered.

The mission achieved a set of firsts: Dragon became the first commercially developed spacecraft to rendezvous with and berth to the ISS, and the first American vehicle to reach the station since the [Space Shuttle program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Shuttle) ended in July 2011.

## Significance

The COTS Demo Flight 2 marked the practical beginning of the commercial spaceflight era. By demonstrating that a private company could safely deliver and return cargo, the flight cleared the way for SpaceX's Commercial Resupply Services contract with NASA; the first operational mission, CRS-1, flew in October 2012. The White House called the achievement one "of historic scientific and technological significance": John P. Holdren, the President's science adviser, issued a statement praising the berthing the day after it occurred.

Coming less than a year after the Space Shuttle's retirement had left the United States dependent on Russian Soyuz and Progress vehicles for station access, the mission restored an American cargo capability and validated NASA's strategy of buying transportation services from industry rather than owning the vehicles outright — a model later extended to crewed flight. The success reinforced 2012 as a landmark year for space exploration, alongside NASA's [Curiosity rover landing on Mars]({{ '/news/science-technology/curiosity-rover-mars-landing/' | relative_url }}) in August and the confirmation that [Voyager 1 was leaving the heliosphere]({{ '/news/science-technology/voyager-1-heliosphere-crossing/' | relative_url }}).

## Key Facts

- **Launch**: 22 May 2012, 07:44 UTC, Falcon 9 v1.0 from SLC-40, Cape Canaveral
- **Mission designation**: COTS Demo Flight 2 (Dragon C2+), under NASA's COTS program
- **Berthing**: 25 May 2012 — grappled by Canadarm2 at 13:56 UTC by Don Pettit and André Kuipers; berthed to the Harmony module
- **Cargo delivered**: ~525 kg pressurized supplies
- **Cargo returned**: ~665 kg, including experiment samples
- **Splashdown**: 31 May 2012, Pacific Ocean off Baja California
- **Firsts**: first commercial spacecraft to berth with the ISS; first U.S. vehicle to reach the station since the Space Shuttle's July 2011 retirement
